About the project

Why I Read

Between the Lines documents the books I have read throughout 2026 and examines the ideas, events and perspectives presented in them.

The aim is not simply to summarise each book, but to consider what it communicates, how it communicates it and how convincing its ideas are.

My approach

Reading beyond the summary

01 · ANALYSIS

Examining the author's perspective

A book presents information from a particular perspective. This is especially important in historical and political writing, where an author's experiences, beliefs and purpose can influence which events are emphasised and how they are explained. I aim to distinguish between evidence, interpretation and possible bias rather than accepting every statement without question.

02 · INTERPRETATION

Connecting ideas to the real world

Books can communicate ideas beyond the events described on their pages. Fiction can explore human behaviour through invented situations, while nonfiction can explain historical events, scientific concepts and political decisions. My reviews consider these ideas and examine what can reasonably be learned from them.

What the reviews consider

More than a plot summary

Each review includes a summary of the book's main content, followed by an evaluation of its ideas and effectiveness. Where relevant, I consider the evidence used, the author's perspective, the significance of particular events and the questions the book raises.

My aim is to give a balanced assessment. A book can contain valuable information while still having limitations, and disagreeing with an author's argument does not automatically make the entire work unhelpful.

Understanding a book means examining not only what it says, but also how its ideas are supported.

The reading footprint

A record of what I have read

This website brings together my reading from 2026, including works on history, politics, science and fiction. Each book offers a different subject to examine, and the reviews record my interpretation of its content.

The collection is also a record of how I evaluate information: identifying significant ideas, considering alternative perspectives and separating an author's claims from the evidence supporting them.
